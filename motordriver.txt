The Hiwonder four-channel encoder motor driver is a module designed to drive TT motors or Hall encoder motors using a microcontroller. Its operating voltage ranges from 3V to 12V direct current, which is determined entirely by the voltage requirements of the connected motors. To ensure the module remains stable, it is strongly advised to avoid using the same power supply for both the driver module and other high-power devices, as this can cause operational instability.

The physical board features several connection interfaces, including four dedicated encoder motor ports. To prevent improper wiring, the board uses two distinct types of connectors for these motors. The 6P-PH2.0/1.25mm port is specifically for connecting TT motors, as their pin sequence differs from standard encoder motors, and connecting them to other ports could cause a short circuit. The 6P-PH2.0/2.0mm port is used exclusively for standard direct current encoder motors. Each motor port contains pins for the negative Hall power supply, the positive Hall power supply which is generally 3.3V or 5V, the A-phase pulse signal, the B-phase pulse signal, and the positive and negative motor power electrodes. The voltage between the motor power electrodes matches the motor's specific requirements. Additionally, when the motor shaft rotates clockwise, the A channel signal precedes the B channel signal, whereas it follows the B channel during counterclockwise rotation.

For communication and power, the board includes an IIC port with clock, bidirectional data, ground, and 5V direct current output pins. There is also a three-pin power port featuring negative, positive, and empty connections, alongside a main power supply port with positive and negative terminals supporting under 36V direct current. When wiring the system, the power supply must correspond to the motor's voltage, and if a third-party battery is used, it should have a discharge rate of at least 3C for reliable performance. Power should be managed either by using separate sources for the main controller and the motor driver, or by utilizing a step-down converter where the driver receives the higher voltage and the controller receives the reduced voltage.

When connecting the module to an Arduino UNO via I2C, the data, clock, and ground pins on the module should connect to the A4, A5, and ground interfaces on the Arduino, respectively. The 5V pin on the I2C port only supports voltage input and cannot output power to the Arduino. Instead, the 5V pin on the driver's serial port should be used for power, though it is important to note that starting from board version V1.4, this pin was changed to a not-connected state to prevent accidental power conflicts. The board's status is indicated by a power indicator light that goes out when off, remains steadily lit during normal operation on versions V1.3 and older, and flickers on version V1.4.

The module is controlled programmatically through several specific registers. Register 0x00 samples the battery voltage. Register 0x14 sets the motor type, with options for a motor without an encoder, a TT encoder motor, an N20 encoder motor, and a JGB encoder motor. Register 0x15 controls the encoder polarity and must be set to zero to prevent the motor from locking into a single rotation direction. Register 0x1F handles open-loop fixed PWM control with a range from negative one hundred to positive one hundred, while register 0x33 manages closed-loop fixed speed control. Finally, register 0x3C tracks the total pulse count for each motor, which can be reset by writing sixteen bytes of zeros to that address.

Users should also be aware of physical differences across board versions. Version V1.2 contains a printing error where the data and clock pin labels are swapped, requiring users to reverse their connections. This labeling issue was corrected in version V1.3. Furthermore, version V1.4 altered the serial port's 5V pin to ensure a more stable power supply.
